## Stay Informed with Weekly Website Activity Reports

**Source:** [https://simple-history.com/support/weekly-email-report/](https://simple-history.com/support/weekly-email-report/)

If you’re like me, you don’t always have time to check what’s happening on your WordPress sites every single day. That’s why I built weekly email reports for Simple History – so you can stay in the loop without constantly logging in to check your dashboard.

## What you get in your weekly email

#### Total events overview

See the total number of events that happened on your site during the week.

#### Activity breakdown by day

A quick view of which days were busiest, so you can spot patterns in your site’s activity.

#### Posts and Pages activity

Track how many new posts were created and how many existing posts or pages were updated.

#### User login activity

See both successful logins and failed login attempts – helpful for spotting potential security issues.

#### Plugin changes

Know when plugins were activated or deactivated on your site.

#### WordPress updates

Get notified when WordPress core updates were completed.

## Why I think you’ll like this feature

**It’s perfect for busy site owners.** I manage several WordPress sites myself, and I don’t always remember to check what’s been happening. Weekly emails solve this problem nicely. Plus, you can preview exactly what the email looks like and even send a test email before enabling it.

**You can spot issues before they become big problems.** If you suddenly see a bunch of failed login attempts, you’ll know about it and can take action. Or if a website owner suddenly starts installing plugins, you’ll see that.

**Great for client reporting too.** If you manage sites for clients, you can add their email to the list and they’ll stay informed without bothering you with “what happened this week?” questions.

## Setting it up is simple

1. Go to your Simple History settings page and find the “Email Reports” section
2. Check the box to “Send an email report with website statistics every Monday morning”
3. Add email addresses in the Recipients field – one email per line (no need for commas)
4. Use “Show email preview” to see exactly what the email will look like
5. Send a test email to make sure everything works correctly
6. That’s it – you’ll get your first report the following Monday

## What the free version sends, and what Premium adds

Right now, everyone who uses Simple History gets a clean weekly summary showing total events, daily activity breakdown, posts and pages activity, user logins (both successful and failed), plugin changes, and WordPress updates. It’s a nice overview that covers the essential activity on your site.

Premium fills in the names. Instead of “12 logins” you see who logged in, and which usernames and IP addresses failed to. Instead of “8 posts updated” you see which posts, and who edited them. Plugin changes come with version numbers, media uploads with file names and uploaders, comments with the posts they were left on, and themes with what was switched or updated. Long lists get truncated so the email stays readable — you see the first few and open the log for the rest.

If you look after sites for other people, this is the one that earns its keep: a Monday email per site that names what changed, which you can read in a minute or forward straight to the client.

## A few common questions

**Will this slow down my site?**
Nope. The emails are generated in the background using WordPress’s built-in scheduling system.

**Can I turn it off if I change my mind?**
Of course! Just uncheck the box in settings, or click the unsubscribe link in any email.

**Do you store my email address somewhere?**
No external storage – everything stays in your WordPress database where it belongs.

**What if I want to send reports to my whole team?**
Just add multiple email addresses, one per line in the Recipients field. Everyone gets the same report.
