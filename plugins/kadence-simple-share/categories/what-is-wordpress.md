## WordPress.com vs WordPress.org

**Source:** [https://www.kadencewp.com/help-center/docs/kadence-general/what-is-wordpress/](https://www.kadencewp.com/help-center/docs/kadence-general/what-is-wordpress/)

WordPress.com and WordPress.org both use the same WordPress software, but they work very differently. WordPress.com hosts and manages your site for you. WordPress.org gives you the free software to install on hosting you choose, with full control over your site. This guide explains the differences so you can pick the right option.

## What is WordPress?

WordPress is free, open source software for building and publishing websites. It powers everything from personal blogs to large business and ecommerce sites. A few terms will come up throughout this guide:

- **Hosting:** a server that stores your website’s files and makes the site available online. Every WordPress site needs hosting.
- **Theme:** controls your site’s overall design, such as layouts, colors, and typography.
- **Plugin:** a software package you install to add features to your site, such as forms, online stores, or courses.
- **Blocks:** the building pieces of the WordPress editor. Each block is one piece of content, like a paragraph, image, button, or gallery.

The software itself is the same everywhere. What differs between WordPress.com and WordPress.org is **who hosts the site** and **how much control you have over it**.

## Key differences at a glance

|  | WordPress.com | WordPress.org (self hosted) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A hosted service that runs WordPress for you | Free software you install on your own hosting |
| Hosting | Included and managed by WordPress.com | You choose and pay for a hosting provider |
| Account needed | A WordPress.com account | No WordPress.com account needed |
| Setup | Sign up and start building in minutes | Install WordPress on your hosting, often with a one click installer from your host |
| Custom domain | Available, depending on your plan | Yes, any domain you own |
| Themes | Choice of themes depends on your plan | Any free or premium theme, or your own |
| Plugins | Installing third party plugins requires a paid plan | Install any plugin |
| Code, files, and database access | Limited, depending on your plan | Full access |
| Updates, security, and backups | Handled by WordPress.com | Your responsibility, though many hosts handle much of it for you |
| Ecommerce and monetization | Available, depending on your plan | No restrictions |
| Support | WordPress.com support, depending on your plan | Your hosting provider, plus the WordPress community |

WordPress.com changes its plans and features from time to time. For what each plan currently includes, check [WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/) directly.

## WordPress.com

[WordPress.com](https://wordpress.com/) is a hosted service that runs WordPress for you. You create an account, choose a site name and design, and start adding content. There is no software to install and no server to manage.

WordPress.com offers a free plan and several paid plans. Free sites come with limits, such as a WordPress.com address (for example, 
```
yoursite.wordpress.com
```

) and fewer design and feature options. Paid plans unlock more, such as a custom domain, more themes, and the ability to install third party plugins and themes.

WordPress.com also adds its own interface on top of WordPress. Some screens may look different from the ones you see in tutorials written for self hosted WordPress.

**Pros**

- Quick, simple setup with nothing to install
- Hosting, software updates, security, and backups are handled for you
- A free plan to get started

**Cons**

- Less control over your site, especially on lower plans
- Third party plugins and themes require a paid plan
- Features and limits are set by WordPress.com and can change

## WordPress.org (self hosted WordPress)

[WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/) is the home of the free, open source WordPress software. There you can download WordPress, read the official documentation, browse the official theme and plugin directories, and join the community that builds it.

WordPress does not run on your personal computer. It needs to be installed on a web server, so you need a hosting provider. Many hosts install WordPress for you, and managed WordPress hosts often also handle updates, backups, and security monitoring.

Because you control the hosting, you control the whole site. You can use your own domain, install any theme or plugin, edit the code, and access your files and database.

**Pros**

- Full control over your site, files, and code
- Use any theme or plugin, free or premium
- No platform limits on design, features, or monetization

**Cons**

- You need to choose and pay for hosting
- You are responsible for updates, security, and backups, though many hosts handle much of this for you
- Setup takes a few more steps than WordPress.com

## Which one should you choose?

The right choice depends on how much control you need and how much you want to manage yourself.

**WordPress.com may suit you if your site is:**

- A personal blog or hobby site
- Something you want to launch quickly without managing hosting
- Simple enough that you will not need custom plugins or code

**Self hosted WordPress (WordPress.org) may suit you if your site is:**

- A business, professional, or project site
- An online store, membership site, or online course
- A site that needs specific plugins, a custom design, or custom code
- A site you plan to grow or earn money from

If you plan to use premium WordPress plugins or themes, check that your setup allows you to install them. On self hosted WordPress you can install any plugin or theme. On WordPress.com, installing third party plugins and themes requires a paid plan that supports it.

## Common questions

### Are WordPress.com and WordPress.org the same company?

No. WordPress.org is the home of the open source WordPress project. WordPress.com is a commercial service owned by Automattic. The WordPress name is a registered trademark of the [WordPress Foundation](https://wordpressfoundation.org/). The two are often confused because WordPress cofounder Matt Mullenweg also founded Automattic.

### How do I know which one I am using?

- If you signed up at WordPress.com and manage your site from your WordPress.com account, you are using WordPress.com.
- If you have an account with a separate hosting provider and WordPress is installed there, you are using self hosted WordPress (WordPress.org).

### Can I move my site from WordPress.com to self hosted WordPress?

Yes. You can export your content from WordPress.com and import it into a self hosted WordPress site. Many hosting providers also offer migration help.

### Is the WordPress software really free?

Yes. The WordPress software is free and open source. With self hosted WordPress, your costs come from hosting, your domain name, and any premium themes or plugins you choose to buy.
